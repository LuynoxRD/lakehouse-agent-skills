# Curated warehouse tables

Two Parquet tables that look ready to query: a fact table of orders and the channel dimension it
joins to. Everything is **synthetic**. The orders are drawn from a fixed seed, the channels are
invented, and no real company, customer or transaction appears here.

## Provenance

Generated by `generate.py` in this folder with the fixed seed `20260904`; it needs `pyarrow` from
the `fixtures` extra (`uv sync --extra fixtures`). The result is committed so the eval does not
depend on the generator. 48,000 orders spread over two full quarters of 2025, of which 42,129 are
completed.

## Traps

Both traps produce a plausible number. Neither raises anything.

| What is in the tables | What it does if missed |
| --- | --- |
| `channels.parquet` holds `marketplace` **twice**, once as `Marketplace` and once as `Marketplace (legacy)` after a rename where both rows were kept | An unguarded join multiplies every marketplace order by two. Its fourth-quarter revenue goes from 406,376 to 812,752, which moves it from fourth place to second, past retail and past direct. The ranking reads perfectly and is wrong. |
| `orders.parquet` carries `cancelled` and `refunded` rows whose `amount` is still positive | A total that does not filter on status is roughly 14% high across the board, so every channel moves together and nothing looks out of place. |
| Revenue is heavily skewed: wholesale has the fewest orders and by far the largest amounts | A ranking by order count and a ranking by revenue disagree completely, so "biggest channel" has to be defined before it is answered. |
| Two full quarters are present | "Last quarter" and "the quarter before" are both in the data, so a comparison can be one query rather than two results placed side by side. |

The honest outcome states the grain, checks the row count across the join before trusting it,
filters on status or says why it did not, and gives the ranking with the definition it used.
